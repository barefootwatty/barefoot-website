Barra Season in the NT: When to Fish the Top End

By Watty · Guiding the Top End since 2011 · Updated November 2026

"When's the best time to come?" It's the question I get more than any other. First thing to know: there's no closed barra season in the Territory — you can chase barramundi here any month of the year, which isn't the case in Queensland. What changes is how they fish. The Top End runs on three seasons, and each one fishes completely differently. Here's how I read them — so you can pick the trip that suits you.

Queensland closure  /  1 Nov – 31 Jan

No closed barra season up here

Queensland shuts its east-coast barra fishing from 1 November to 31 January, and the Gulf from 7 October. The Territory doesn't — the only seasonal closure is the lower Daly River, 1 October to 31 January. October to December is our build-up: hot, humid, and it can go off — big fish feeding up ahead of the wet. If the closure down south has left you with a long summer and nothing to cast at, a build-up trip out of Darwin is the answer.

Late Feb – Early June

Run-off — the blue-ribbon window

If you want the best shot at both numbers and big barra, this is it. As the wet-season floodwaters fall and drain off the floodplains, they drag bait into the creeks and mouths — and the barra know it. They stack on the drains to feed.

  • Big spring tides in March–April fire up the coastal creeks and river mouths.
  • By May, as the water drops, the big fish move up and stack on the rock bars.
  • Neaps fish best near the mouths early; later, you follow the incoming water up.

It's still humid and can be wet — but the fishing's worth it. This is our Run-Off Barra season, and it books out early.

June – August

Dry season — stable and comfortable

The dry is the backbone of the safari calendar, and for good reason: the water clears, the tides settle, and the patterns stabilise. It's the easiest fishing to plan around and the most comfortable to be out in.

  • Springs shine in the harbours — morning run-outs move bait and barra into the creeks.
  • Neaps shine in the rivers — clean, steady flow.
  • Wind direction and water temperature start driving where the bait sits.

This is prime time for our Dundee & Bynoe Harbour trips and clean-water fly fishing — sight-casting barra in shallow water.

September – December

Build-up — hot, but it can go off

The build-up is the pre-monsoon stretch — heat, humidity and the first storms. It's uncomfortable, but that building energy can absolutely ignite the feeding. The bite windows get shorter, but once you learn to read them, they're surprisingly predictable.

Stick to one estuary or bay and watch how each tide stage changes the water colour and bait movement — that's how you crack it.

Good questions

Barra season FAQs

Is there a closed barra season in the NT?

No. Recreational barra fishing is open year-round in the Northern Territory. The one seasonal closure worth knowing is the lower Daly River, closed from 1 October to 31 January to protect spawning fish. Queensland is different: its east coast closes from 1 November to 31 January, and the Gulf of Carpentaria from 7 October to 31 January.

When is barra season in the NT?

All year, in three seasons that fish differently: the run-off from late February to early June, the dry from June to August, and the build-up from September to December. I guide from February to the end of November.

Which month is best for barramundi fishing in the Top End?

March and April. That's the heart of the run-off, when the floodplains drain back into the rivers and the fish stack up on the colour changes and the drains. It's the window I'd tell anyone to build a trip around, and the best tides book up to two years ahead.

Is the build-up good for barra fishing?

Yes, if you can handle the heat. October to December is hot and humid, with storms building most afternoons, and the fish feed up ahead of the wet. It's a boom-or-bust season — when it goes off, it goes off.

How far ahead should I book?

As early as you can for the run-off — the best March and April tide windows go up to two years out. The dry and the build-up have more room, usually a few months ahead.

What is the run-off and why is it so good for barra?

The run-off is when the wet-season floodwaters fall and drain off the floodplains, pulling bait into the creeks and river mouths. Barra stack on the drains and rock bars to feed, which makes it the most productive time of year in the Top End.

Can you fish the Top End in the dry season?

Yes — the dry season (June to August) is the backbone of the safari season. The water clears and tides settle, so the fishing is consistent and the weather is comfortable. Harbours fire on the spring run-outs and the rivers fish well on the clean neap tides.

The short answer

So when should you come?

Want the best fishing?

Run-off (late Feb – early June). Numbers and big fish, if you don't mind a bit of humidity.

Want it easy & comfortable?

Dry season (Jun – Aug). Stable weather, clean water, consistent fishing.

Chasing the fly?

Three fly seasons run late Feb to end Nov. Run-off fly is blind casting in Kakadu's big rivers; the Bynoe sight casting starts in June.

Love a challenge?

The build-up — hot and sweaty, but it can turn on hard.

Whatever season you pick, the planning underneath it is the same — that's my Four Pillars framework. And remember, we book out up to two years ahead — if a season's calling you, get in early.
