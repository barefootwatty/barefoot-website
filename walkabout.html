Try finding another pair of rods that does this.

The tips swap between the spin and the baitcast.

  • Every rod comes with a spare tip.
  • Buy one of each and you’ve got four tips between two rods.
  • Break a tip and you grab the spare and keep going.

It wasn’t a popular request with the engineers, that’s for sure. We worked on it until we found a guide set that works on both, and you’d never pick the difference unless I told you.

Nobody made the rod I actually wanted.

G’day, I’m Watty from Barefoot Fishing Safaris in Darwin. I’ve been guiding for barra up here since 2011, and I’ve caught fish in more than twenty countries.

I also run Angling Adventures, so four or five times a year I’m on a plane to fish somewhere else in the world. I host groups, check out new places and fish with the lodges, so I need rods that can do a bit of everything.

I’ve used some really good rods over the years, but there was always something missing.

  • One of them you couldn’t get parts for, so if you broke a piece, that was the whole rod gone.
  • I never found one in the length and action I want to fish with all day, every day.
  • I wanted a spin rod and a baitcaster that share the same tips, and nobody was doing it.

So this is the Walkabout.

Rods break. With a Walkabout, that’s not the end of your trip.

Sometimes it’s on a fish, but a lot of the time it’s before you’ve even made a cast. It’s the car door, the ceiling fan, a tree, or your mate dropping the esky on it.

  • You grab the spare tip and keep going.
  • Every section can be replaced, so you buy it once and you’ve got it for life.
[PHOTO walkabout-boat: rod on the boat or behind the seat of the ute]

It goes in your checked bag, so it turns up when you do.

Rod tubes are always the first thing to get left off the plane. When you’ve got three or four flights, then a charter into somewhere remote, a lost rod tube is a very big problem.

  • It packs down to four pieces.
  • Each piece sits in its own slot in the neoprene rod cover, with the spare tip in a rigid tube.
  • You’re not using up your baggage on a rod tube.

It’s not a travel rod. It’s a seriously good fishing rod you can take anywhere.

[PHOTO walkabout-packed: four pieces in the rod cover, sitting in an open travel bag]

Hot days up here, seven international flights, and two weeks in northern Mongolia at minus ten.

Back home they’ve caught barra, saratoga, jewfish and queenies, and we had a fifty-one fish session in Bynoe on the smallest lures in the box.

What a mate would ask before buying one

Does a four-piece rod fish as well as a one-piece?

My best fly rods are four-piece and sell for close to $2,000. Piece count isn’t the problem. Build quality is.

What will it fish?

Barra, saratoga, jewfish, queenfish, taimen, black bass, peacock bass and more. It’s not for marlin or bream.

Do the tips really swap?

Yes. The tips share one guide setup across the spin and the baitcast.

What’s postage?

Australia: free for Founding rods. Otherwise $25, or $30 Express.
New Zealand: $45.
Papua New Guinea: $75.
Rest of the world: $80. The buyer pays any import taxes.

When does it ship?

The rods are in Darwin and every one is checked before it ships. Order by 10 December for Parcel Post before Christmas.

How does the warranty work?

Register the Purchase ID card from the box on the Barefoot website. Registered owners also get the Fuji guide codes for spare guides.
