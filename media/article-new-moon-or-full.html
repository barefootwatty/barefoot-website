Darwin barra bite times: new moon or full, does it matter?

Which moon do you book a barra trip around, the new or the full? On the Darwin chart the two put the highs and the lows at near enough the same times. The difference is the night. One's lit and the other's pitch dark.

In June the full moon is straight overhead at about one in the morning, riding high over Darwin, with the low low, the lower of the day's two lows, underneath it. Two weeks later, on the new moon, it's about one in the morning again and the low low again, and it's pitch dark. I go through the tide chart every year, and this year we counted every tide in the 2026 table. On the clock there's a few minutes between the two moons.

Side by side

The majors are the moon straight overhead or straight underfoot, and the minors are moonrise and moonset. On the new moon the majors are at quarter to one, in the middle of the night and again in the middle of the day, and on the full they're a few minutes later. The tide is on the bottom with them. On the new moon the highs are at half past six morning and evening and the minors at about seven, and on the full it's a few minutes later again.

So on either moon you get a high in the morning, the run-out through the middle of the day with the low and the major at lunch, and the top of the tide back at dusk with the minor. Both moons bring the springs, the big tides, so that run-out is a fast one, and the morning high tends to be the one out of the wind, before the afternoon breeze gets up.

A lit night and a dark one

On the full moon the sun goes down and the moon comes up inside the next 50 minutes, with the evening high topping out 20 to 45 minutes ahead of it, so dusk is the top of the tide and the minor together. Then the moon climbs, and it's straight overhead at around one with the spring low under it. It's still up at sunrise and it sets on the morning high. You get that on the night of the full moon and one night either side.

On the new moon the moon is up with the sun and down with the sun, so the night is dark right through. The minors are still at about seven, morning and evening, and the one major in between is at around one with the moon underfoot and the tide on the bottom.

During the day it's the other way round and you'd never know. At lunch on the new moon the major is the moon at the top of its climb, and you can't see it because it's up there with the sun. At lunch on the full moon it's underfoot.

Which moon rides high

I've been telling people on the course for years that the low low switches in late August, from the middle of the night to the middle of the day. That comes down to where the moon sits in the sky. Through the dry the full moon rides high over Darwin and the new moon sits low in the north, and through the wet it's the other way round. That swing is what makes one of the day's two lows lower than the other and decides which one it is.

It's the same on both moons. On the springs the low low is at night from April to August and during the day from September to February. So in June that one o'clock low low is in the dark on the new moon and under the full moon a fortnight later, and from September the low low is the lunchtime one on both, and that's the tide that drains the flats.

A week later

A week after either moon it's a different day altogether. The majors are at dawn and dusk, the low is at twenty to five, and you're coming onto the neaps, the small tides. It all comes about 50 minutes later every day, so a week past either moon the whole clock has moved, and between the new and the full there's only a few minutes in it.

So I don't pick between them. The back of the moon, a day or two after either one, is my favourite, because that's when the tides are at their biggest, and those days get booked out first every year.

Tide first

The moon doesn't put a fish in the boat on either night. All of this tells you where the water and the moon will be against the clock. Tide first, then wind and water clarity, and the moon bite times third.

Open the chart at this month's new moon and the full and put the lows side by side. If you've got a spot you only fish on one moon, tell me which moon and why. I'm a bit of a tide chart nerd, but that's one I can't get off the chart.
